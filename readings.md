# Core Belief ORB

An original educational synthesis. Five overlapping contexts are a navigation device, not a validated continuum of harm. All 25 examples, their possible functions, and alternative phrasings are hypothetical author interpretations, not quotations, diagnosis, prevalence estimates, or demonstrated causal histories. Source notes distinguish conceptual background from supporting evidence. No personal assessment or tracking data is collected.

## 1. When an assumption becomes an identity

Orientation · home

A short sentence can quietly organize a life: “I am a burden.” “People are unsafe.” “My value is what I produce.” A core belief is a broad conclusion about the self, other people, or the world. A rule tells you how to live as if that conclusion were true.

This orb follows the passage from experience to interpretation, from interpretation to a protective rule, and from a repeated rule to a seemingly fixed identity. The passage is a useful model, not an inevitable sequence. Beliefs can also precede an event, change later in life, or remain flexible.

Enter through one of five contexts. They overlap: a convention can be enforced through affection, a workplace can generate persistent stress, and a controlling relationship can be traumatic. Their order does not rank suffering. The same sentence can have several histories—or no traumatic history at all.

**Question to consider:** Which is the useful distinction here: what happened, what someone concluded, or the rule they now feel obliged to follow?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf), [NIMH · Traumatic events and PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)

## 2. Social convention

Context · convention

Some assumptions arrive through ordinary praise, jokes, traditions, images, and expectations. A convention becomes questionable when a local preference is treated as a universal test of human worth.

Notice the difference between “People here often do this,” “People here expect this,” and “I am wrong if I do not do this.” These are different claims. A freely chosen tradition can support belonging; the same practice can become restrictive when dissent carries penalties.

The examples below are an interpretive collection, not findings about a particular culture. Ask whose values the convention serves, whether it is negotiable, and whether it fits the person expected to follow it.

Sources / conceptual background: [Van Grootel et al. (2018) · Pluralistic ignorance](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01344/full)

## 3. Conditional belonging

Context · belonging

A relationship may appear to offer acceptance only when someone is useful, agreeable, successful, or easy to care for. In that setting, a person might begin organizing life around keeping the connection.

This branch explores possible meanings of conditional acceptance. It does not infer neglect or abuse from a personality style, and it does not label every family expectation harmful. The relevant questions concern consistency, reciprocity, room for difference, and the consequences of having needs.

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf), [HealthyWA · Assertive communication](https://www.healthywa.health.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Assertive-communication)

## 4. Persistent stress

Context · stress

Long periods of strain can narrow what seems possible. These examples concern uncertainty, over-responsibility, repeated evaluation, and limited resources. They describe possible interpretations, not a diagnosis or a single biological mechanism.

Keep the external conditions in view. Financial insecurity, disability-related barriers, discrimination, and unreasonable workloads may be real. A useful distinction is between a particular constraint and a total judgment about a person’s competence, worth, or future.

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf), [VA · Helpful thinking after disasters](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/disaster_events/for_everyone/helpful_thinking.asp)

## 5. Traumatic threat

Context · trauma

Trauma can affect beliefs about safety, trust, responsibility, and personal value. People respond differently, and most people exposed to dangerous events do not develop PTSD. No sentence in this collection establishes a diagnosis.

This branch separates the reality of what happened from conclusions that extend to every person, every situation, or an entire identity. It never requires deciding that the world is safe. Present danger deserves practical attention, and reflection can wait when someone feels overwhelmed.

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Cognitive Processing Therapy](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/cognitive_processing.asp), [NIMH · Traumatic events and PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)

## 6. Coercive influence

Context · coercion

Undue influence is used here descriptively for pressure that substantially constrains independent choice. Coercive control can involve repeated isolation, threats, humiliation, or control of resources. The legal definition depends on jurisdiction and relationship; this orb does not decide whether a legal threshold is met.

For partners, families, workplaces, or groups, examine conduct: access to outside information, freedom to disagree, material dependence, and the cost of leaving. An unfamiliar worldview or a strong commitment alone is not evidence of coercion.

The short statements in this branch are authored illustrations. Agreement under pressure does not establish free consent. If retaliation is possible, do not use a confrontation or abrupt withdrawal as a belief-testing exercise; confidential specialist support may be more appropriate.

Sources / conceptual background: [UK Home Office · Controlling or coercive behaviour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework-accessible)

## 7. My worth is my output.

Core belief · rest

A setting praises constant activity and treats rest as something to justify.

**Associated rule:** I must earn every pause.

**Possible protective purpose:** Staying busy may offer recognition or a clear way to count contribution.

**When it becomes rigid:** Human worth becomes difficult to feel during illness, caregiving, unemployment, or an ordinary quiet afternoon.

**A more flexible possibility:** Contribution matters to me; my worth need not rise and fall with today’s output.

**Question to consider:** Would I apply the same standard to someone whose work is invisible?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · Perfectionism resources](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Perfectionism)

## 8. My feelings make me weak.

Core belief · emotion

A person repeatedly hears that sadness, fear, or tenderness is embarrassing.

**Associated rule:** I must remain composed to deserve respect.

**Possible protective purpose:** Concealing emotion may protect against ridicule in that setting.

**When it becomes rigid:** The rule can turn ordinary feeling into an additional reason for shame.

**A more flexible possibility:** A feeling can be uncomfortable without being a verdict on my strength.

**Question to consider:** Is the judgment about the feeling itself, its expression, or the audience?

Sources / conceptual background: [Van Grootel et al. (2018) · Pluralistic ignorance](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01344/full)

## 9. My body must earn acceptance.

Core belief · body

Appearance is treated as a public measure of discipline, morality, or value.

**Associated rule:** I must look acceptable before I can be seen.

**Possible protective purpose:** Managing appearance may promise social ease or fewer unwanted judgments.

**When it becomes rigid:** Participation becomes conditional on reaching a moving visual standard.

**A more flexible possibility:** Appearance can affect how people treat me; it does not settle what care I deserve.

**Question to consider:** Whose standard is operating, and who is excluded by it?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf)

## 10. I am behind in life.

Core belief · timeline

One timetable for education, work, partnership, or family is presented as adulthood itself.

**Associated rule:** I must reach the usual milestones on schedule.

**Possible protective purpose:** Following the timetable may provide orientation and social recognition.

**When it becomes rigid:** A different life course becomes evidence of failure instead of information about circumstances or preference.

**A more flexible possibility:** A shared timetable is one reference point; it is not a complete evaluation of my life.

**Question to consider:** Does this milestone reflect a chosen value, an available opportunity, or an inherited deadline?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · Adjusting rules and assumptions](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Improving-Self-Esteem/Improving-Self-Esteem---07---Adjusting-Rules-and-Assumptions.pdf)

## 11. Needing help makes me lesser.

Core belief · independence

Self-sufficiency is celebrated while dependence is mocked or made invisible.

**Associated rule:** I should manage everything alone.

**Possible protective purpose:** Doing things alone may preserve pride and avoid obligations that feel uncertain.

**When it becomes rigid:** Ordinary interdependence starts to feel like a personal defect.

**A more flexible possibility:** I can value competence and still use support suited to the situation.

**Question to consider:** Which forms of help are already hidden inside what people call independence?

Sources / conceptual background: [HealthyWA · Assertive communication](https://www.healthywa.health.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Assertive-communication)

## 12. I am lovable only when useful.

Core belief · lovable

Attention seems warmest when a person performs a service or solves someone else’s problem.

**Associated rule:** I must keep giving to keep my place.

**Possible protective purpose:** Usefulness may become a predictable route to connection.

**When it becomes rigid:** Receiving care without immediately repaying it can feel undeserved or precarious.

**A more flexible possibility:** Usefulness is one thing I offer; a reciprocal relationship can contain more of me.

**Question to consider:** What distinguishes appreciation for a contribution from affection contingent on it?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf)

## 13. My needs are too much.

Core belief · needs

Requests are repeatedly met with impatience, comparison, or withdrawal.

**Associated rule:** I must ask for as little as possible.

**Possible protective purpose:** Reducing requests may preserve connection or reduce conflict.

**When it becomes rigid:** Other people cannot respond to needs they never get to hear, and unmet needs can be mistaken for proof of unworthiness.

**A more flexible possibility:** A need can be real even when a particular person cannot meet it.

**Question to consider:** Can the legitimacy of a need be separated from the availability of a response?

Sources / conceptual background: [HealthyWA · Assertive communication](https://www.healthywa.health.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Assertive-communication)

## 14. Disagreement costs me love.

Conditional assumption · conflict

Difference is followed by coldness or threats to the relationship.

**Associated rule:** I must agree before I understand what I want.

**Possible protective purpose:** Agreement may keep the interaction predictable in the moment.

**When it becomes rigid:** Consent becomes hard to distinguish from fear of disconnection.

**A more flexible possibility:** A disagreement can reveal a difference; what follows tells me about this relationship.

**Question to consider:** Is there room for both people to remain present while wanting different things?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How to say no assertively](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Assert%20Yourself/Assert%20Yourself%20-%2006%20-%20How%20to%20Say%20No%20Assertively.pdf)

## 15. A flaw makes me unacceptable.

Core belief · perfect

Approval seems to disappear when performance is merely adequate.

**Associated rule:** I must be beyond criticism.

**Possible protective purpose:** Extra preparation may reduce exposure to criticism.

**When it becomes rigid:** Success gives temporary relief while the standard for being acceptable keeps moving.

**A more flexible possibility:** A flaw is information about a part of my performance, not the whole of my person.

**Question to consider:** What would count as enough if acceptance were not the prize?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · Perfectionism resources](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Perfectionism)

## 16. Everyone’s feelings are my job.

Core belief · caretaker

Someone becomes accustomed to monitoring the emotional climate around them.

**Associated rule:** I must prevent other people from becoming upset.

**Possible protective purpose:** Anticipating moods may reduce surprises or preserve a valued role.

**When it becomes rigid:** Care turns into a duty to control outcomes that no person can fully control.

**A more flexible possibility:** I can care about someone’s feelings without being responsible for every feeling they have.

**Question to consider:** Where does influence end and another person’s agency begin?

Sources / conceptual background: [HealthyWA · Assertive communication](https://www.healthywa.health.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Assertive-communication)

## 17. Without control, I cannot cope.

Core belief · controlall

Repeated disruptions make preparation feel like the only dependable resource.

**Associated rule:** I must anticipate every possible problem.

**Possible protective purpose:** Planning can reduce real uncertainty and offer direction.

**When it becomes rigid:** The plan expands until uncertainty itself looks like failure.

**A more flexible possibility:** I can prepare for some things and respond to others with the resources available.

**Question to consider:** Is this preparation proportionate to a current risk, or an attempt to remove all uncertainty?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Helpful thinking after disasters](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/disaster_events/for_everyone/helpful_thinking.asp)

## 18. One mistake will undo me.

Conditional assumption · mistake

A workplace, school, or household reacts to small errors as if they define the person.

**Associated rule:** I must check until no error is possible.

**Possible protective purpose:** Checking may genuinely catch errors and reduce immediate criticism.

**When it becomes rigid:** No finite amount of checking can guarantee a flawless future; the person may become unable to finish.

**A more flexible possibility:** Consequences vary. I can distinguish repairable errors from high-stakes ones.

**Question to consider:** What is the actual consequence of this particular error, and what repair is available?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · Perfectionism resources](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Perfectionism)

## 19. I am a burden.

Core belief · burden

Resources are stretched and a person notices that their needs require time, money, or attention.

**Associated rule:** I must hide the cost of caring for me.

**Possible protective purpose:** Concealment may seem to spare others strain or prevent rejection.

**When it becomes rigid:** The existence of a cost becomes confused with a judgment that the person should not need care.

**A more flexible possibility:** Care takes resources. That fact alone does not make my existence a problem.

**Question to consider:** Would the same resource shortage make another person undeserving?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem develops](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Improving%20Self-Esteem/Improving%20Self-Esteem%20-%2002%20-%20How%20Low%20Self-Esteem%20Develops.pdf)

## 20. There will never be enough.

World belief · scarcity

Repeated shortages make loss an immediate practical concern.

**Associated rule:** I must hold on to everything and never relax.

**Possible protective purpose:** Conserving resources can be sensible and necessary.

**When it becomes rigid:** An unqualified “never” can erase differences between present scarcity, future uncertainty, and areas where some choice remains.

**A more flexible possibility:** Some resources are limited now; I can name the shortage without predicting every future.

**Question to consider:** What is known about the present shortage, and what remains uncertain?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Helpful thinking after disasters](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/disaster_events/for_everyone/helpful_thinking.asp)

## 21. Nothing I do makes a difference.

Agency belief · future

Attempts repeatedly meet obstacles, arbitrary decisions, or exhausted resources.

**Associated rule:** There is no point in trying.

**Possible protective purpose:** Expecting little may protect against another round of disappointment.

**When it becomes rigid:** A broad conclusion can hide smaller forms of influence while leaving the original barriers unexplained.

**A more flexible possibility:** Some outcomes are outside my control; other actions may still matter at a smaller scale.

**Question to consider:** Which obstacle is external, and which small outcome could an action actually affect?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Helpful thinking after disasters](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/disaster_events/for_everyone/helpful_thinking.asp)

## 22. Nowhere is safe.

World belief · unsafe

A dangerous event makes threat difficult to put into a bounded place and time.

**Associated rule:** I must stay alert everywhere.

**Possible protective purpose:** Alertness may help detect danger and avoid being surprised.

**When it becomes rigid:** If every setting is treated as the same setting, restorative moments may become difficult to recognize.

**A more flexible possibility:** Safety is specific and imperfect; I can assess this place, these people, and this moment.

**Question to consider:** What is a current sign of danger, and what is a reminder of a different event?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Trauma reminders](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/what/trauma_triggers.asp)

## 23. I have no say.

Agency belief · powerless

Someone has been unable to stop an event or protect a boundary.

**Associated rule:** My preferences do not change anything.

**Possible protective purpose:** Expecting little agency may make an uncontrollable situation easier to predict.

**When it becomes rigid:** A past absence of choice can become an assumption about choices that exist now.

**A more flexible possibility:** My options may be limited; I can distinguish a past lack of control from a present choice.

**Question to consider:** Where is choice actually available today, however small?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Cognitive Processing Therapy](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/cognitive_processing.asp)

## 24. It happened because I am bad.

Core belief · blame

A person searches for an explanation of harm and locates the cause in their identity.

**Associated rule:** I must become good enough to prevent it happening again.

**Possible protective purpose:** Self-blame may seem to offer a rule for preventing another event; that is a possible meaning, not a universal reason.

**When it becomes rigid:** Responsibility for an event is replaced by a global moral verdict.

**A more flexible possibility:** What I knew, could choose, and could control then matters; harm does not prove badness.

**Question to consider:** What facts concern responsibility, and what words turn those facts into a judgment of identity?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Cognitive Processing Therapy](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/cognitive_processing.asp)

## 25. I am permanently damaged.

Core belief · damaged

Distress or changes after an event are interpreted as evidence of an irreversibly altered self.

**Associated rule:** I must not expect a different life.

**Possible protective purpose:** Lowering expectations may seem to protect against hope followed by disappointment.

**When it becomes rigid:** The word “permanently” makes a prediction that today’s experience cannot establish.

**A more flexible possibility:** What happened may have lasting effects; today does not tell me every possibility ahead.

**Question to consider:** Which effects are present now, and which conclusions predict the rest of a life?

Sources / conceptual background: [NIMH · Traumatic events and PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)

## 26. Closeness leads to harm.

Conditional assumption · trust

Someone who mattered also caused harm, failed to protect, or broke trust.

**Associated rule:** I must keep everyone at a distance.

**Possible protective purpose:** Distance may reduce exposure to a known kind of hurt.

**When it becomes rigid:** A boundary that protects from one person may become a rule excluding all possible connection.

**A more flexible possibility:** Trust can be specific, gradual, and revisable; closeness does not have to be all or nothing.

**Question to consider:** What conduct would justify a small amount of trust, and what would justify withdrawing it?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Negative coping and PTSD](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/gethelp/negative_coping.asp)

## 27. My judgment cannot be trusted.

Core belief · judgment

A person’s observations are repeatedly dismissed while one authority claims exclusive interpretive power.

**Associated rule:** I must let them decide what is real.

**Possible protective purpose:** Deferring may reduce dispute and keep access to a relationship or group.

**When it becomes rigid:** Checking one’s view against independent evidence becomes confused with disloyalty.

**A more flexible possibility:** My judgments can be mistaken and still deserve consideration alongside independent evidence.

**Question to consider:** Who is allowed to question an interpretation, and what happens when they do?

Sources / conceptual background: [UK Home Office · Controlling or coercive behaviour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework-accessible)

## 28. A good person obeys.

Rule treated as identity · obedience

Moral status is made conditional on following an authority’s direction.

**Associated rule:** I must comply even when I have serious doubts.

**Possible protective purpose:** Compliance may preserve approval or avoid a penalty.

**When it becomes rigid:** A legitimate disagreement becomes a supposed defect of character.

**A more flexible possibility:** Commitment can include questions; obedience alone does not establish goodness.

**Question to consider:** Can the instruction be examined without attacking the person who questions it?

## 29. Only this group can keep me safe.

World belief · outsiders

Outside relationships or sources are presented as uniformly corrupt or threatening.

**Associated rule:** I must avoid people and information outside it.

**Possible protective purpose:** Staying inside may offer belonging and a ready-made explanation of uncertainty.

**When it becomes rigid:** The claim becomes harder to evaluate when it forbids access to anything that could challenge it.

**A more flexible possibility:** Safety claims need evidence that can be checked beyond the authority making them.

**Question to consider:** What independent information would be allowed to count against this claim?

## 30. I owe them my freedom.

Core belief · debt

Past help, sacrifice, or rescue is invoked as an unlimited claim on present choices.

**Associated rule:** Because they helped me, I cannot refuse.

**Possible protective purpose:** Repayment may preserve gratitude and avoid conflict over an obligation.

**When it becomes rigid:** A specific debt becomes an open-ended surrender of consent.

**A more flexible possibility:** Gratitude and finite obligations can be real without granting ownership of my choices.

**Question to consider:** What was actually agreed, and does the claimed obligation have any limit?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How to say no assertively](https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/~/media/CCI/Consumer%20Modules/Assert%20Yourself/Assert%20Yourself%20-%2006%20-%20How%20to%20Say%20No%20Assertively.pdf)

## 31. My boundaries are not mine.

Core belief · owned

Access is demanded as proof of loyalty, love, or membership.

**Associated rule:** They are entitled to my time, body, money, or privacy.

**Possible protective purpose:** Compliance may reduce immediate conflict or retaliation in a constrained situation.

**When it becomes rigid:** Permission is treated as permanently given, leaving little room to withdraw or renegotiate it.

**A more flexible possibility:** A relationship does not make consent permanent or erase the importance of boundaries.

**Question to consider:** Is refusal meaningfully possible, or is permission being presumed under pressure?

## 32. Experience & conditions

Mechanism · experience

Start with a bounded description: who did what, under what conditions, and what choices existed? A fact about treatment by others is different from a conclusion about the worth of the person treated.

Illustrative contrast: “My request was refused today” describes an event. “My needs never count” extends it across situations. Repetition may give that extension more weight, but the two statements still answer different questions. Conditions can need changing even when the broader conclusion is questioned.

**Question to consider:** What would this explanation clarify—and what would it leave out—in one of the illustrative beliefs?

## 33. The meaning assigned

Mechanism · meaning

An interpretation answers “What does this say about me, people, or the world?” The same event can support competing interpretations. Their plausibility depends on evidence and context, not simply on which one sounds kinder.

For example, a delayed reply might mean someone is busy, uncertain, avoiding contact, or something else. “I do not matter” adds a global self-evaluation. The point is to notice that added claim before treating it as the only possible reading.

**Question to consider:** What would this explanation clarify—and what would it leave out—in one of the illustrative beliefs?

Sources / conceptual background: [CCI · How low self-esteem is maintained](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/-/media/CCI/Consumer-Modules/Improving-Self-Esteem/Improving-Self-Esteem---03---How-Low-Self-Esteem-is-Maintained.pdf)

## 34. The protective rule

Mechanism · rule

Rules often take the form “I must…” or “If I…, then…”. They translate a broad belief into instructions: if worth depends on achievement, keep achieving; if disagreement predicts rejection, stay agreeable.

A rule can be locally useful yet too rigid for every setting. Examine what it asks, what it promises, and when it can be revised. Calling it protective describes a possible function; it neither proves how it originated nor requires keeping it forever.

**Question to consider:** What would this explanation clarify—and what would it leave out—in one of the illustrative beliefs?

## 35. The loop that keeps it plausible

Mechanism · loop

A belief can influence what gets noticed and how an ambiguous event is interpreted. A protective response may then reduce opportunities to encounter an exception. This is one maintenance model, not an explanation for every enduring belief.

Illustrative loop: expect disapproval → conceal a preference → avoid an immediate disagreement → never learn whether this person could accept a difference. But a hostile response can also be real evidence about that relationship. Inspect the setting before assuming the only problem is interpretation.

**Question to consider:** What would this explanation clarify—and what would it leave out—in one of the illustrative beliefs?

## 36. From a sentence to “who I am”

Mechanism · identity

Here, “identifying with a belief” means that a conclusion has become a default self-description. Compare “I made an error in this task” with “I am incompetent.” The second statement reaches much further.

In this orb, rigidity means a belief resists relevant exceptions, crosses contexts without checking, or punishes questioning. This is an explanatory definition, not a scored psychological measure. Frequency, conviction, emotional intensity, and practical consequences are different dimensions.

## 37. A belief with room to revise

Mechanism · flexibility

A useful alternative is specific enough to be credible and open enough to update. “Everyone is safe” is another absolute. “I can assess trust person by person” leaves room for both caution and connection.

Begin with one statement, distinguish current conditions from broad predictions, and consider a relevant exception. Reflection is optional; remembering or reliving a traumatic event is not required. Where serious distress persists, a qualified clinician can help with an approach matched to the person.

Changing a belief need not mean tolerating a harmful situation. Safer conditions, practical support, and changes in relationships or institutions may matter alongside a change in interpretation. A visitor can read this collection purely as an observer.

**Question to consider:** What would this explanation clarify—and what would it leave out—in one of the illustrative beliefs?

## 38. Can a sentence tell us its origin?

Knowledge floor · floor-origin

Life experiences can influence self-evaluation, but a present belief does not identify a unique past event or cause.

A present-day statement is insufficient. This is a limit of inference from this orb, not a declaration that all individual histories are unknowable. Voluntarily supplied context and longitudinal observations could add information; a suggested story or prompted memory is not proof.

**Evidence boundary:** Inference limit

**Question to consider:** What evidence would distinguish social learning, repeated relational pressure, and interpretation after a discrete event for a particular person?

## 39. Old alarm, present danger, or both?

Knowledge floor · floor-context

Trauma reminders can produce distress; coercive or materially unsafe conditions can also be ongoing.

The orb cannot observe the setting or assess safety. Independent information and appropriate professional or specialist support may help. Do not “test” a belief by exposing someone to retaliation. This is a practical assessment gap, not an unsolved universal research question.

**Evidence boundary:** Context not assessed

**Question to consider:** What current evidence separates a generalized prediction from a realistic appraisal of this setting?

Sources / conceptual background: [VA · Trauma reminders](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/what/trauma_triggers.asp), [UK Home Office · Controlling or coercive behaviour](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework/controlling-or-coercive-behaviour-statutory-guidance-framework-accessible)

## 40. Why does change differ between people?

Knowledge floor · floor-change

Effective PTSD treatments exist, and research continues on differences in symptoms, recovery, and treatment components.

The sources support this broader research area, not an exact prediction for an individual or for every belief in this collection. Prospective studies and treatment studies that measure belief change alongside context could sharpen the question. No particular method is claimed here to resolve it completely.

**Evidence boundary:** Active research; limited prediction

**Question to consider:** Which combinations of personal history, present conditions, and intervention explain why a belief shifts readily for one person and persists for another?

Sources / conceptual background: [NIMH · Traumatic events and PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd), [VA · Cognitive Processing Therapy](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/cognitive_processing.asp)

## 41. What would “rigid” mean in a study?

Knowledge floor · floor-measure

This orb distinguishes conviction, generalization, willingness to update, and costs. It has not validated an instrument that combines them.

Before making comparisons, define the dimensions and examine reliability, cultural meaning, and validity. A useful study might compare responses across contexts and over time. This is a design question for this framework, not a claim that existing psychological measures do not exist.

**Evidence boundary:** Unvalidated authoring framework

**Question to consider:** Could two people endorse the same statement yet differ sharply in flexibility, distress, and freedom of choice?
